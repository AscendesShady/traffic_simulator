## Findings

### C-01 — Consumed decisions have no freshness gate (Low)

`main.merge_ai_decision()` applies `decision.json` every 30 frames with no check on its age.
`guard.safe_decision()` writes a `timestamp`, but `main.py` reads that field only to fill an Excel
column (`main.py:216`) — never to gate application.

Probe result: a decision stamped **24 hours old** was applied as `status=OK`.

This inverts the doctrine stated in `agent.py:358` — *"stale or missing telemetry cannot authorize
priority"*. The agent applies staleness to its **input** (`STALE_SECONDS = 3.0`); the simulator
applies none to its **output**. If the agent process dies while flags are on, the last decision keeps
being re-applied indefinitely and the panel keeps showing `OK`. No test covers decision age; the
agent-side staleness tests cover telemetry only.

*Suggested fix:* reject or hold-all-off a decision older than a few tick intervals.

### C-02 — Route lock releases at PRIORITY_CLEARING (Low)

`agent.check_locked()` locks a route only while its nearest bus is in `actionable_buses()`, which
requires `leg_state == "APPROACHING"`. A bus leaves `APPROACHING` at the same moment the arbiter
moves `PRIORITY_ACTIVE → PRIORITY_CLEARING` (both keyed on `is_front_bumper_upstream`). The
"preserve GRANTED routes" rule therefore stops protecting a grant during its clearing phase, so a
model turn can flip flags mid-grant.

Degrades safely: `_request_is_live()` sees `FEATURE_DISABLED` and exits through `RECOVERY_ALL_RED`.
Effect is a needlessly cancelled grant, not a conflict.
