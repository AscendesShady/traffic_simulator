# TSP/DBL scenario matrix on the 500 m network — 2026-09-24

What the first scenario matrix on the lengthened network (500 m link, 350 m
approaches) showed about calibration, the maximum cycle, TSP and DBL; the
defect it exposed (DBL was coupled to the node's single TSP slot); and four
defects the pre-experiment check found (a left-turner could pivot from
between two lanes; headless rule arms decided on another run's telemetry;
headless summary rows reported no priority; rule arms were not deterministic
under CPU load).

## Setup

- 60 headless runs of 15 simulated minutes (`headless_run.run`, 300 s warm-up
  discarded), seeds 234 and 764, speed scale 0.6017.
- Demand: the 2026-09-22 campaign rates (EB 38, WB 35, A_NB 26, A_SB 29,
  B_NB 27, B_SB 24 veh/min) × 0.4, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8 and 1.0.
- Arms: baseline, TSP, DBL, TSP+DBL on the default TSP routes; 100 m
  eligibility zone (a 350 m variant at 0.6× and 0.8×); maximum cycle 120 s,
  with 150 s and 180 s at 0.7×, 0.8× and 1.0×.
- Bus headways were the defaults of the time, 30 / 45 / 90 / 30 / 45 / 90 s
  (R1–R6). They were changed the same day to 180 / 240 / 300 / 240 / 240 /
  180 s, so the bus-side figures below describe the old service frequency.
- Every run's offered demand was identical across arms of one seed
  (`demand_draw_hash`), so each arm-minus-baseline difference is paired.

## Calibration

The calibration record of every regime is in
`results/calibration_report_scenarios.html` (generated by
`src/telemetry/calibration_report.py`).

- Saturation flow S = 1,396 veh/h/lane from 25 uninterrupted headways of a
  30-vehicle standing queue (median 2.30 s, mean 2.58 s; none excluded as
  interrupted), 13 % heavy vehicles. Start-up lost time l1 = 1.79 s,
  measured. Yellow 3.0 s and all-red 3.5 s (ITE at v85 = 43.5 km/h,
  W = 35.5 m); lost time 12.58 s per cycle.
- Webster at Node A:

  | Demand | Y | C0 | Cycle used (cap 120 s) | X |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0.4× | 0.47 | 45 s | 45 s | 0.65 |
  | 0.6× | 0.70 | 80 s | 80 s | 0.83 |
  | 0.7× | 0.82 | 132 s | 120 s | 0.92 |
  | 0.8× | 0.94 | 371 s | 120 s | 1.05 |
  | 1.0× | 1.17 | undefined | 120 s | 1.31 |

- In-network saturation flow (stop-bar headways during the run) was 0.93–1.04
  of the calibrated S on all 60 runs. Latent demand exceeded 5 % of offered
  on every 0.8× and 1.0× run except one (0.8× at a 180 s cap) and on one of
  four 0.7× runs at 120 s: those regimes are past practical capacity, not
  miscalibrated. At 0.8× no cycle can bring X below 1 short of
  L / (1 − Y) = 197 s.

## Maximum cycle

Baseline arm, mean of the two seeds, total person-hours of delay including
the boundary wait:

| Demand | 120 s | 150 s | 180 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.7× | 419 | 376 (−10 %; runs its own 132 s optimum) | 376 |
| 0.8× | 499 | 464 (−7 %) | 461 (−0.7 %) |
| 1.0× | 696 | 677 (−3 %) | 675 (−0.3 %) |

Bus delay rose with the longer reds (0.8×: 123 → 136 → 134 pax-h). The
default maximum cycle was raised to 150 s (`webster.MAX_CYCLE_SEC`,
`global_config["max_cycle_sec"]`), within the Signal Timing Manual's range for
large intersections; 180 s bought nothing measurable. The golden outputs were
re-pinned for this change alone (both scenarios reproduced the old
fingerprints exactly at 120 s).

## TSP

- At 0.4× and 0.6× (45 s and 80 s cycles) TSP barely acts: 100–115 requests
  per run, 4–7 grants, all green extensions, no early greens. Per run,
  requests end with the bus crossing on green anyway (48–57), timing out
  (22–35) or with the red too short to cut past the 6.5 s clearance (6–15).
- At 0.8× (120 s cycle) TSP acts: 12–13 early greens and 4–5 extensions per
  run. On both seeds bus time loss per trip fell by 20–25 s, car on-road delay
  rose by 28–36 pax-h, boundary wait fell by 34–67 pax-h and vehicles served
  fell by 6–7 %; the total including boundary wait moved −26 and +5.

## DBL

In all six DBL-only pairs the boundary wait rose (by 5–39 pax-h) with latent
demand: left-turn arrivals are retained at the source while the lane is
reserved.

## Defect: DBL waited for the node's TSP slot

**Symptom.** DBL requests collapsed in the TSP+DBL arm (57 → 15.5 at 0.4×,
33.5 → 4 at 0.8×). Lane 2 was reserved for 44–318 s per run, against
3,000–4,800 s in the DBL-only arm.

**First hypothesis, partly wrong.** A bus that checks in for TSP before
reaching lane 2 holds a TSP-only request, and `_collect_priority_requests`
skipped it once it became DBL-eligible. The upgrade was added (the request
takes DBL on and moves its entry lane, `tests/test_signal_priority.py::
test_a_tsp_request_takes_on_dbl_when_its_bus_reaches_lane_2`), but a re-run of
the TSP+DBL arm was bit-identical: at the 100 m zone every bus reaches lane 2
long before the zone. The upgrade matters for zones that begin before the
merge, such as the 350 m option.

**Trace.** Per bus-leg tracing (0.4×, seed 234, 5 min): in the combined arm 26
of 40 bus-legs gave DBL up before the zone, against 13 DBL-only. At 10 of 14
give-ups the node's single armed slot was held by another bus's TSP-only
request and DBL was not active on the bus's approach; in 11 a left-turner was
standing ahead of the bus in lane 2. DBL requests waited 5,772 frames behind
TSP-only requests (and, DBL-only, 16,603 frames behind other buses' DBL
requests, often on the opposite approach).

**Cause.** DBL was granted only to the node's armed request, the one TSP
slot. Buses that had given DBL up kept raising TSP requests that held the slot
for up to the 120 s request timeout; the waiting bus's lane stayed
unprotected, left-turners refilled it, and the next bus gave DBL up too.

**Fix.** DBL is granted when requested and holds lane 2 on the bus's own
approach while its request lives, armed or queued
(`SignalController._dbl_requests`, read by `is_dbl_active_for_approach`,
`get_active_dbl_request` and `get_all_dbl_states`). Several buses can hold one
approach, so a car that cannot vacate holds only when it is ahead of none of
them (`dbl_buses_for_approach`). Telemetry's `dbl_active_triggered` covers
every live DBL request and `dbl_priority_pending` is always false. TSP keeps
one action per node at a time. Test:
`test_dbl_holds_its_lane_while_another_bus_holds_the_tsp_slot`.

**Consequence.** DBL-only and TSP+DBL results produced before this fix measure
the coupled mechanism.

## Defect found in the pre-experiment check: a turner pivoting from a straddle

After the bus headway defaults changed, the test suite's AI Configured soak
(`test_scripted_plan_drives_the_whole_network_without_overlaps`, seed 7)
reported one overlap inside Node A's box at frame 8,540: an EB through car in
lane 1 and an EB left-turner that had just pivoted north. The turner had been
told to vacate lane 2 about 380 px before the bar because the planner had
commanded that lane (the eviction exemption covered only turners within
250 px); its slide froze half-way (y 1,355.4 between the lane-2 and lane-1
centres of 1,345 and 1,367) for over 1,000 frames, still labelled lane 2
because a slide changes `lane_index` only on completion. The "hold until in
lane 2" check read that label, so the car entered the box and pivoted from
the straddle across lane 1. The overlap appeared with the new headways
whether DBL was coupled or not and never with the old headways: the headways
changed the traffic, the defect was already there.

*Fix* (`vehicle.py`, step 1 and step 1d): a vehicle still sliding out of lane
2 counts as out of it, is held and slides back unless a bus's DBL keeps it
out; a commanded lane, which is shared with left-turners, never evicts one.
Test: `test_a_turner_half_way_out_of_lane_2_is_held_and_turns_from_lane_2`
(fails without the fix). The golden scenarios now pin the former headways
(`GOLDEN_HEADWAYS`) so their 127 s window still contains priority events; with
that pin both reproduce their previous fingerprints exactly, so neither this
fix nor the DBL decoupling changed them.

## Defect found in the pre-experiment smoke campaign: rule arms on stale telemetry

A smoke campaign through the phase-one pipeline (baseline, rule-based and
passenger-pressure-tsp × seeds 234 and 764, 10 minutes, results kept out of
`results/`) completed cleanly -- one `config_hash`, identical demand per
seed, pairs formed, calibration report written -- but both rule arms raised
no TSP or DBL request, and every paired difference was exactly 0.0. All 60
decisions per run were `HELD_ALL_OFF` (`guard_reject_rate` 1.0,
`decisions_effective` 0). The turn log showed why: each turn's telemetry
snapshot was hours old, from another run (a different `run_uuid`, a 120 s
cycle, S = 1,278 from speed scale 0.5). `agent._read_telemetry` and
`read_ai_control` took `path=TELEMETRY_PATH` / `AI_CONTROL_PATH` as default
arguments, evaluated at import, so `parallel_campaign._isolate_runtime`
redirected the module constants to no effect and every in-process rule turn
read the repository's own `data/traffic_state_telemetry.json`. The windowed
app never redirects these paths and was unaffected; the only rule-arm rows in
`results/` (campaign 2026-09-22) came from it. The test suite's isolation
redirects them too, so its agent tests had also been reading that real file.

*Fix:* both readers resolve the path when called. The campaign test now
asserts the rule arm's `guard_reject_rate` is 0.0 (with the old binding it
reads 1.0). Only these two functions in `src/` took a runtime path as a
default argument.

The re-run smoke campaign then showed the rule arms deciding (reject rate
0.0, 18–26 effective decisions per run) and the bus event logs showed the
treatment (DBL served at 8 node crossings and one TSP early green in the
rule arm, seed 764), yet the summary rows still reported zero TSP and DBL
requests. `_controller_experiment_metrics` read the controller from
`main._active_signal_controller`, which only the windowed `main()` set; a
headless run fell back to all-zero metrics (and the default 60-frame yellow).
*Fix:* `perform_full_reset` registers the run's controller. Test:
`test_a_headless_run_summarises_its_own_controller`.

With rule turns accepted, the parallel = sequential campaign test became
flaky (it failed two runs in four). `agent.ai_turn` recorded a rule turn's
measured compute time as `latency_ms`, and `main._decision_release_frame`
releases a decision that many frames after its snapshot: a few milliseconds
is one frame, but under CPU load (two workers side by side) some turns took
long enough to land a frame later, and the run diverged. *Fix:* a rule turn
carries zero latency. Test: `test_a_rule_turn_carries_no_latency` (30.6 ms
without the fix); the campaign test then passed three runs in three.

## Noise

At 0.4× the TSP arm made four short extensions, yet its total delay moved +8
and +19 pax-h against baseline: trajectory divergence after a small
perturbation, not an effect. At 15 minutes, paired differences under about
±10–20 pax-h are not resolvable with two seeds. The matrix shows mechanisms
(grants, early greens, lane time, bus time loss per trip); effect sizes need
the 60-minute campaign with the replication count `print_campaign_summary`
reports.
